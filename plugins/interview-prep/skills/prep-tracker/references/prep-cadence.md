# When to do what

Preparation compresses badly. Three weeks of work does not fit into the
afternoon before the round, and the parts that need spacing — stories,
vocabulary, design practice — are exactly the parts people leave until then.

## Three weeks out (or as soon as a loop exists)

- The loop is written down, with every round tagged stated / inferred / unknown.
- The recruiter has been asked about anything tagged unknown.
- The story bank exists and `stories.py check` has been run once. Gaps here take
  the longest to fix, because they need recall, not study.
- The top three risks are written as prep items with dates.

## One week out

- One mock per technical round in the loop, spaced across the week — not two in
  one evening. The second mock in a sitting measures stamina, not skill.
- Every weak point from a mock becomes a prep item with a date before the round.
- The company research is done and written down: what they build, how they make
  money, something recent and specific, and two questions you actually want
  answered.
- Rehearse the 90-second opener out loud, once.

## 48 hours out

- Re-run only the round that graded weakest, with different questions.
- Read the stories that match this round's competencies. Read, do not rewrite.
- Confirm the logistics: time zone, link, who is on the call, how long, what
  they expect you to have prepared.
- Stop learning anything new. A technology encountered for the first time two
  days before a round produces a confident wrong answer, which is worse than
  "I have not used it".

## The day before

- Nothing new. Re-read the brief, the loop and the four stories. Twenty minutes.
- Sleep is the highest-leverage preparation left, and this is not a figure of
  speech for a live coding round.

## Two hours before

- Re-read the job description once. It is the vocabulary the interviewer will
  use.
- Have the numbers to hand: your range, your notice period, your availability.
- Have your two questions written down where you can see them.

## Within two hours after

Debrief, while it is still verbatim. What was asked, what you said, where you
stalled, what they seemed to be probing for, and anything they told you about
the next round. This is the single highest-value thirty minutes in the whole
process and it is the one everybody skips.

## Across a whole search

- Mocks decay: a round mocked more than three weeks ago and not repeated counts
  as unprepped.
- Stories improve with use. After every real behavioural round, update the ones
  that were asked with what actually landed.
- Two loops at once is manageable; four is not, and the tracker will show it as
  a wall of overdue items rather than as a decision to make.
