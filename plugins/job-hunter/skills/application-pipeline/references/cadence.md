# Follow-up cadence

Defaults, not rules. The user's judgement and the employer's stated process
both override them — if a posting says "we reply within three weeks", the date
is three weeks, not four days.

## After applying or bidding

| Situation | Chase after | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct application, no acknowledgement | 5 working days | once more after 10, then close |
| Contract bid on a platform | 3 working days | platforms move faster; a silent bid at 7 days is lost |
| Application through a recruiter | 3 working days | the recruiter is the channel; chase them, not the client |
| Referral from someone you know | 5 working days | chase the referrer once, gently, then stop |

Two chases is the limit on an unanswered application. A third is noise, and it
is remembered.

## After a conversation

| Situation | Next action | Date |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any interview or call | thank-you note with one concrete follow-through | same day |
| They said "we'll be in touch this week" | chase | first working day after that week ends |
| They gave no timeline | ask for one, in the room | before the call ends |
| Take-home submitted | chase | 5 working days |
| Final stage, no answer | chase, and say you are managing other timelines | 5 working days |

## When another offer is live

Tell every open pipeline row that has reached screening or later, with the real
deadline. It is the one message that reliably accelerates a process, and
withholding it costs the user leverage they already earned.

## Closing

Close a row when: they said no; you said no; two chases went unanswered; or the
posting is filled or withdrawn. Record which — a pipeline that shows *why*
things ended is the only data a search generates about itself.

Six weeks of silence is a no. Record it as one and move on.

## What not to automate

Do not send anything. Draft the follow-up, show it to the user, and let them
send it. The cadence table decides *when* something is due; it never decides
that a message goes out.
