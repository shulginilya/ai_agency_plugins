# Reading a posting

The script counts keywords. These are the things that predict how the
engagement actually goes, and they are only visible to a reader.

## Green

- **The work is described, not the culture.** A specific system, a specific
  problem, a named constraint. Whoever wrote it has met the job.
- **Scope is one role.** One seniority, one discipline, a bounded surface.
- **The process is stated** — how many stages, who you meet, how long.
- **Money is named**, or a band is, without being asked.
- **The tech list is short.** Five things they use beats twenty they have heard
  of; a long list usually means nobody technical wrote the ad.
- **For contracts: a start date, a duration and an extension basis.**

## Amber — ask before investing

- **Two jobs in one ad.** "Backend engineer who will also own our data
  platform and mentor the team." Ask which one is being measured.
- **A wide seniority band** (mid to staff). Means the level, and the rate, are
  decided after they meet you.
- **"Fast-paced", "wear many hats", "startup mentality"** with no concrete
  work. Not fatal, but the ad is doing no filtering, so neither can you.
- **No named interviewer or team.** Common with agencies re-posting a client's
  role; you may be one of forty CVs sent blind.
- **A take-home before any human conversation.** Fine if the role is worth it;
  price the hours into the decision.
- **For contracts: no mention of duration, or "possibility of permanent"** used
  as compensation for a low rate.

## Red

- **A deal-breaker from the profile.** The script catches the ones you declared;
  read for the ones you have not.
- **Rate below floor, framed as opportunity.** Exposure, equity-only, "great
  for your portfolio".
- **The scope grows as the ad goes on.** Six responsibilities by paragraph
  four is a role nobody has defined.
- **Unpaid work in the process** — a real deliverable presented as a test.
- **Re-posted for months.** Either the role is not real or nobody survives it.
- **Contract framed to dodge classification rules** — a full-time employee's
  obligations at a contractor's risk. Worth flagging to the user; the rules
  differ by country and this is a question for them, not for you.

## Two questions worth asking every time

1. **What does the first ninety days look like?** Everyone who has thought
   about the role can answer this. Nobody who has not, can.
2. **Why is the position open?** Growth, replacement, or a role that keeps
   turning over are three different jobs.

## What none of this tells you

Whether they will hire you. Triage is about not spending a day on a posting
that was never going to work — it is not a probability estimate, and it should
never be reported as one.
